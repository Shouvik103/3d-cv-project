THE RECONSTRUCTION JOURNEY
From photographs to a surface
  1. 01Prepare
    Orient photos, preserve calibration and choose a matching size.
  2. 02Find cameras
    Match reliable features, register views and refine poses with BA.
  3. 03Estimate depth
    Choose calibrated SGBM stereo or monocular depth aligned to sparse geometry.
  4. 04Fuse & inspect
    Filter inconsistent depth, fuse a TSDF surface, inspect holes and export.

      
What the viewer is showing
A point cloud is a set of colored 3D samples. A mesh connects vertices into triangles. Choose about 60 suitable views covering every side; uploads support up to 200. More photos help only when their camera poses and overlap are reliable.
The point preview displays up to 115,000 samples. Object-quality classical clouds use accepted depth estimates, capped at one million exported samples; the TSDF surface cloud is a separate download. Caps do not create points or fill unseen sides. Hover coordinates use a normalized display frame with arbitrary units.
High and Ultra increase depth resolution and the TSDF grid. They can resolve more detail, but cannot reconstruct unseen sides or repair incorrect camera geometry.
How we judge a reconstruction
Read registration, camera error and pose AUC together. Low reprojection error alone can accompany incorrect global geometry.
For surfaces, read accuracy together with completeness, F-scores, coverage and topology. Attractive colors can hide holes and distorted geometry.
